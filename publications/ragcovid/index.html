Time Series Forecasting Using RAG-based News Information and Large Language Models: A Case Study on COVID-19 Data

Dongkuk Kim1*, Yeonjun Choi1*, Beakcheol Jang1

1Yonsei University

*Equal contribution

JICS

Numerical forecasting models and existing LLM-based forecasting cannot reflect external factors such as policy changes or cluster outbreaks. We retrieve and summarize news from the three days before the target date and add it to the LLM input. On daily COVID-19 cases in South Korea, this produced lower errors than numerical models such as LSTM and Transformer and than LLM forecasting without news.

Abstract

Time series forecasting is widely used across industrial and public sectors. In particular, forecasting the spread of COVID-19 plays a crucial role in planning quarantine policies. Traditional numerical forecasting models effectively learn from historical data but struggle to incorporate external factors such as social events or policy changes. In rapidly evolving situations like a pandemic, it is also difficult to secure sufficient high-quality time series data. Recently, studies have attempted to use large language models (LLMs) for time series forecasting. However, most rely on pre-trained internal knowledge, which limits their adaptability to external changes. This study proposes a new LLM-based forecasting method that integrates external information to address these limitations. Using the Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approach, we retrieve COVID-19-related news from the three days preceding the target date. These documents are summarized and converted into a natural language template, which is then used as input to the LLM. Experiments using daily confirmed COVID-19 case data in South Korea show that the proposed framework consistently outperforms traditional models such as LSTM, TCN, Transformer, and Informer, as well as existing LLM-based methods. By refining and integrating unstructured external information into the LLM input, this approach offers a new direction for time series forecasting. It also shows the potential for broader applications beyond infectious disease prediction, including domains such as economics, climate, and healthcare.

Motivation

Numerical forecasting models (LSTM, TCN, Transformer, Informer) take only past values as input, so they cannot capture sudden changes driven by external events. Existing LLM-based forecasting methods such as PromptCast and TimeRAG rely on pre-trained knowledge or on past numerical patterns, and cannot reflect information that appears after training. COVID-19 case counts change sharply with social distancing measures and cluster outbreaks, which makes them a suitable test of whether external information improves forecasting.

Method

  • Data. Daily confirmed cases from the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (Jan 2020 – Aug 2023). The test period is Aug 2022 – Aug 2023.
  • News database. Articles on COVID-19 cases from Naver News, up to 50 per day, stored in a Milvus vector database.
  • Retrieval and summarization. For each of the three days before the target date, the top 3 articles are retrieved with bge-m3 (9 in total), and GPT-4o-mini summarizes each into at most two sentences.
  • Input. The last 14 days of case counts are converted into a natural-language template and given to GPT-4o-mini together with the summaries, which predicts the next day’s count.
  • Conditions. Six LLM conditions (news or no news, raw or summarized news, zero-shot or few-shot) were compared with four numerical models.

Results

All four conditions that used news outperformed the four numerical models on both RMSE and MAE, and summarizing the news reduced errors further.

Compared with the LLM without news, summarized news reduced RMSE by 15.3% and MAE by 11.5%. Summaries condense the key information and reduce the noise of long articles in the input.
